## Staleness

`content/sources/discover-canada.json` carries **no `knownStaleness` entry naming this chapter**, so
`volatile` here is my own judgement, fact by fact, and it discriminates. Nine facts below are marked
volatile: every one is a population count, a share or a "today" demographic statement, which is what the
schema says the flag is for — a fact that can change without notice. Dated history (1604, 1755–63, 1763,
2006, 2008) is stable and marked so.

No monarch is named or gendered anywhere in this bank. The one Crown reference this chapter needs is
historical — the Royal Proclamation of 1763 — and a monarch two and a half centuries dead is not the
Sovereign; W-11 still routes the answer to the *year and the document* rather than to the name, so nothing
in an option or explanation depends on who was reigning.

## Indigenous content

`docs/content-review.md` §9.4 governs. Questions that faithfully paraphrase what *Discover Canada* says
ship; questions that state anything beyond it do not, and adding context is not mine to add. §9.2 lets the
guide's own terms ("Aboriginal peoples", "Aboriginal and treaty rights") stand where the question is about
the text of the guide or the Constitution; elsewhere the specific names are used. §9.3 forbids putting
living peoples in the past tense — that rules out two facts below on wording grounds alone, and they are
flagged rather than reworded, because rewording them would mean adding a time anchor the source does not
have.

## Section 1 — Canada and its founding peoples (p. 16)

### W-01 — A strong and free country
- **Fact:** Canada is known around the world as a strong and free country, and Canadians are proud of their
  own identity.
- **Where:** opening paragraph, p. 16
- **Evidence:** "Canada is known around the world as a strong and free country."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** none sharp; used as a warm-up item.

### W-02 — The oldest continuous constitutional tradition
- **Fact:** Canadians have inherited the oldest continuous constitutional tradition in the world.
- **Where:** p. 16
- **Evidence:** "We have inherited the oldest continuous constitutional tradition in the world."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** Canada is assumed to be a young country with a young constitution, because 1867 sounds
  recent next to Europe.

### W-03 — The only constitutional monarchy in North America
- **Fact:** Canada is the only constitutional monarchy in North America.
- **Where:** p. 16
- **Evidence:** "We are the only constitutional monarchy in North America."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** Mexico or a Caribbean country is guessed, or Canada is thought to share the status
  with the United States.

### W-04 — Peace, Order, and Good Government
- **Fact:** Canada's institutions uphold a commitment to Peace, Order, and Good Government. The phrase comes
  from the British North America Act, Canada's original constitutional document of 1867.
- **Where:** p. 16
- **Evidence:** "a key phrase in Canada's original constitutional document in 1867, the British North
  America Act"
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** Confused with the American "life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness", which is the
  distractor that does the work.

### W-05 — The "Great Dominion"
- **Fact:** Poets and songwriters have called Canada the "Great Dominion".
- **Where:** p. 16
- **Evidence:** "poets and songwriters have hailed Canada as the “Great Dominion.”"
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** Read as an official constitutional title rather than as what writers called the
  country.

### W-06 — Three founding peoples
- **Fact:** To understand what it means to be Canadian, the guide says it is important to know about three
  founding peoples: Aboriginal, French and British.
- **Where:** p. 16
- **Evidence:** "know about our three founding peoples— Aboriginal, French and British."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Terms:** the guide's term, §9.2, because the question is about what this text says.
- **Misconception:** Two founding peoples are named (French and British), leaving the first out — which is
  the exact erasure §9.4 says omitting these questions would cause.

### W-07 — Migration from Asia
- **Fact:** The ancestors of Aboriginal peoples are believed to have migrated from Asia many thousands of
  years ago.
- **Where:** p. 16
- **Evidence:** "The ancestors of Aboriginal peoples are believed to have migrated from Asia many thousands
  of years ago."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** Europe or a much shorter timescale is guessed.

### W-08 — Well established before Europeans arrived
- **Fact:** They were well established here long before explorers from Europe first reached North America.
- **Where:** p. 16
- **Evidence:** "They were well established here long before explorers from Europe first came to North
  America."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** European arrival is imagined as the start of settled life in Canada.

### W-09 — **FLAGGED, NOT AUTHORED** — what First Nations cultures were rooted in
- **Where:** p. 16, the sentence closing the ABORIGINAL PEOPLES section.
- **Why not authored:** The source states it in the past tense with no date. Turning it into a question puts
  a living people's beliefs in the past tense, which `docs/content-review.md` §9.3 forbids; the only fixes
  are to add a time anchor the source does not have (agent-added context, §9.4) or to rewrite the claim.
  Neither is mine. Flagged, not softened, not silently dropped.

## Section 2 — Aboriginal peoples (p. 17)

### W-10 — Aboriginal and treaty rights are in the Constitution
- **Fact:** Aboriginal and treaty rights are part of the Canadian Constitution.
- **Where:** p. 17
- **Evidence:** "Aboriginal and treaty rights are in the Canadian Constitution."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** Believed to rest on policy or on individual agreements rather than on the Constitution.

### W-11 — The Royal Proclamation of 1763
- **Fact:** Territorial rights were first guaranteed by the Royal Proclamation of 1763, which set the basis
  for negotiating treaties with newcomers.
- **Where:** p. 17
- **Evidence:** "Territorial rights were first guaranteed through the Royal Proclamation of 1763 by King
  George III, and established the basis for negotiating treaties with the newcomers"
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** Dated to Confederation in 1867, or to the Constitution Act, 1982.

### W-12 — The treaties were not always respected
- **Fact:** The guide says those treaties were not always fully respected.
- **Where:** p. 17
- **Evidence:** "treaties that were not always fully respected."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** A reader assumes the guide claims the treaties were honoured. It does not, and
  softening this would be exactly the editorialising `OQ-REVIEW-9` rules out — in the other direction.

### W-13 — Residential schools
- **Fact:** From the 1800s until the 1980s the federal government placed many Aboriginal children in
  residential schools.
- **Where:** p. 17
- **Evidence:** "From the 1800s until the 1980s, the federal government placed many Aboriginal children in
  residential schools"
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** The period is imagined as short and long ago, rather than continuing into the 1980s.
- **Authoring note:** the question asks *when*, which is the fact. The guide's statement of *purpose*
  ("to educate and assimilate them") is left in the inventory and not made into an answer: an option
  reading like a justification is a framing judgement, and that is not an author's to make. See W-14.

### W-14 — **FLAGGED, NOT AUTHORED** — the stated purpose of the residential schools
- **Where:** p. 17, same sentence as W-13.
- **Why not authored:** the only faithful option is the guide's own statement of intent, and putting it in
  the correct-answer slot has the game teach a purpose as an answer. Softening it breaks faithfulness;
  keeping it makes the game assert it. `OQ-REVIEW-9` says say so and skip — so it is said, and skipped.

### W-15 — Languages and cultural practices were prohibited
- **Fact:** In the residential schools, Aboriginal languages and cultural practices were mostly prohibited.
- **Where:** p. 17
- **Evidence:** "Aboriginal languages and cultural practices were mostly prohibited."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** The schools are imagined as ordinary boarding schools.

### W-16 — The 2008 apology
- **Fact:** In 2008 the federal government formally apologized to the former students of the residential
  schools.
- **Where:** p. 17
- **Evidence:** "In 2008, Ottawa formally apologized to the former students."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** Confused with the 2006 apology for the Chinese Head Tax or the 1988 apology for the
  wartime treatment of Japanese Canadians — all three are in this guide, which is what makes the year the
  question.

### W-17 — Achievements today
- **Fact:** In Canada today, Aboriginal peoples have renewed pride and confidence and have achieved a great
  deal in agriculture, the environment, business and the arts.
- **Where:** p. 17
- **Evidence:** "have made significant achievements in agriculture, the environment, business and the arts."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** The guide's account is read as ending in the past.

### W-18 — Three groups
- **Fact:** In this guide the term *Aboriginal peoples* covers three distinct groups: First Nations, Inuit
  and Métis.
- **Where:** p. 17–18
- **Evidence:** "Today, the term Aboriginal peoples refers to three distinct groups:"
- **Stability:** stable
- **Terms:** the guide defines "Indian" in this passage; the authored question asks for the three groups and
  answers with First Nations, Inuit and Métis, so §9.1's rejected term never appears in an option.
- **Misconception:** Two groups are named, or "Métis" is thought to be a First Nation.

### W-19 — Where First Nations people live
- **Fact:** About half of First Nations people live on reserve land in roughly 600 communities; the other
  half live off reserve, mostly in cities.
- **Where:** p. 17
- **Evidence:** "about half of First Nations people live on reserve land in about 600 communities while the
  other half live off-reserve, mainly in urban centres."
- **Stability:** **volatile** — a share and a community count.
- **Misconception:** Almost everyone is assumed to live on reserve.

## Section 3 — Inuit, Métis, and shares (p. 18)

### W-20 — What "Inuit" means
- **Fact:** *Inuit* means "the people" in the Inuktitut language. The Inuit live in small, scattered
  communities across the Arctic.
- **Where:** p. 18
- **Evidence:** "The Inuit, which means “the people” in the Inuktitut language, live in small, scattered
  communities across the Arctic."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** The word is thought to name a place, or to be a word from another language.
- **Note:** present tense in the source and present tense in the question (§9.3).

### W-21 — Knowledge of land, sea and wildlife
- **Fact:** Knowledge of the land, the sea and wildlife allowed the Inuit to adapt to one of the harshest
  environments on earth.
- **Where:** p. 18
- **Evidence:** "Their knowledge of the land, sea and wildlife enabled them to adapt to one of the harshest
  environments on earth."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** Survival in the Arctic is credited to European equipment.

### W-22 — Who the Métis are
- **Fact:** The Métis are a distinct people of mixed Aboriginal and European ancestry. Most live in the
  Prairie provinces.
- **Where:** p. 18
- **Evidence:** "The Métis are a distinct people of mixed Aboriginal and European ancestry, the majority of
  whom live in the Prairie provinces."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** Thought to live mainly in Quebec because of the French connection.

### W-23 — Michif
- **Fact:** The Métis come from both French-speaking and English-speaking backgrounds and speak their own
  dialect, Michif.
- **Where:** p. 18
- **Evidence:** "They come from both French- and English-speaking backgrounds and speak their own dialect,
  Michif."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** Michif is guessed to be a French dialect, or an Inuit language.

### W-24 — The three shares
- **Fact:** About 65% of Aboriginal people are First Nations, 30% are Métis and 4% are Inuit.
- **Where:** p. 18
- **Evidence:** "About 65% of the Aboriginal people are First Nations, while 30% are Métis and 4% Inuit."
- **Stability:** **volatile** — census shares.
- **Misconception:** The three groups are assumed to be roughly equal in size.

### W-25 — **FLAGGED, NOT AUTHORED** — the "Unity in Diversity" box on p. 18
- **Where:** p. 18, the Governor General quotation and its picture caption.
- **Why not authored:** the caption describes the man wearing a Blood (Kainai First Nation) headdress.
  `docs/content-review.md` §5.2 puts headdresses on the presumed-restricted list, and even though a question
  is text and not art, any question drawn from this box sits on that caption and the only other content is
  two sentences of direct quotation that an answer would have to reproduce. Skipped on both counts.

## Section 4 — English and French (p. 19)

### W-26 — The two official languages
- **Fact:** English and French are Canada's official languages, and the federal government must provide
  services across Canada in both.
- **Where:** p. 19
- **Evidence:** "The federal government is required by law to provide services throughout Canada in English
  and French."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** Bilingual service is thought to be optional, or to apply only in Quebec and New
  Brunswick.

### W-27 — Anglophones and Francophones
- **Fact:** Canada has about 18 million Anglophones — people whose first language is English — and about
  seven million Francophones, whose first language is French.
- **Where:** p. 19
- **Evidence:** "Today, there are 18 million Anglophones—people who speak English as a first language—and
  seven million Francophones—people who speak French as their first language."
- **Stability:** **volatile** — population counts.
- **Misconception:** The words are thought to describe where someone lives rather than their first language.

### W-28 — Francophones outside Quebec
- **Fact:** Most Francophones live in Quebec, but about a million live in Ontario, New Brunswick and
  Manitoba, with smaller numbers in other provinces.
- **Where:** p. 19
- **Evidence:** "one million Francophones live in Ontario, New Brunswick and Manitoba, with a smaller
  presence in other provinces."
- **Stability:** **volatile** — population count.
- **Misconception:** French speakers are assumed to live only in Quebec.

### W-29 — New Brunswick
- **Fact:** New Brunswick is the only officially bilingual province.
- **Where:** p. 19
- **Evidence:** "New Brunswick is the only officially bilingual province."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** Quebec is guessed, or Ontario, or "all of them".

### W-30 — The Acadians
- **Fact:** The Acadians are the descendants of French colonists who began settling in what are now the
  Maritime provinces in 1604.
- **Where:** p. 19
- **Evidence:** "The Acadians are the descendants of French colonists who began settling in what are now the
  Maritime provinces in 1604."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** Confused with Quebecers, or dated to the same year as Champlain's Québec fortress
  (1608).

### W-31 — The deportation, 1755–1763
- **Fact:** Between 1755 and 1763, during the war between Britain and France, more than two-thirds of the
  Acadians were deported from their homeland.
- **Where:** p. 19
- **Evidence:** "Between 1755 and 1763, during the war between Britain and France, more than two-thirds of
  the Acadians were deported from their homeland."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** A small number is assumed, or the event is dated to the Conquest of 1759 alone.

### W-32 — The Great Upheaval
- **Fact:** That deportation is known as the Great Upheaval. The Acadians survived it and kept their own
  identity.
- **Where:** p. 19
- **Evidence:** "Despite this ordeal, known as the “Great Upheaval,” the Acadians survived and maintained
  their unique identity."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** The name is attached to the wrong event — the Conquest, or the Rebellions of 1837–38.

### W-33 — Quebecers
- **Fact:** Most Quebecers are French-speaking and descend from about 8,500 French settlers of the 1600s and
  1700s, and they maintain their own identity, culture and language.
- **Where:** p. 19
- **Evidence:** "Most are descendants of 8,500 French settlers from the 1600s and 1700s and maintain a
  unique identity, culture and language."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** A much larger founding population is assumed.

## Section 5 — Becoming Canadian (p. 20)

### W-34 — The Québécois are a nation within a united Canada
- **Fact:** In 2006 the House of Commons recognized that the Québécois form a nation within a united Canada.
- **Where:** p. 20 (the sentence begins on p. 19 and the quoted passage is the part on p. 20)
- **Evidence:** "recognized in 2006 that the Quebecois form a nation within a united Canada."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** Read as recognition of a separate country, which is the distractor.

### W-35 — Anglo-Quebecers
- **Fact:** A million Anglo-Quebecers have a heritage of 250 years and are a lively part of Quebec.
- **Where:** p. 20
- **Evidence:** "One million Anglo-Quebecers have a heritage of 250 years and form a vibrant part of the
  Quebec fabric."
- **Stability:** **volatile** — a population count.
- **Misconception:** Quebec is assumed to be entirely French-speaking.

### W-36 — What newcomers are expected to embrace
- **Fact:** In becoming Canadian, newcomers are expected to embrace democratic principles such as the rule
  of law.
- **Where:** "Becoming Canadian", p. 20
- **Evidence:** "In becoming Canadian, newcomers are expected to embrace democratic principles such as the
  rule of law."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** Read as a language or a residence requirement rather than a commitment to principles.

### W-37 — Who established the English-speaking way of life
- **Fact:** The basic way of life in English-speaking areas was established by hundreds of thousands of
  English, Welsh, Scottish and Irish settlers, soldiers and migrants from the 1600s to the 20th century.
- **Where:** p. 20
- **Evidence:** "established by hundreds of thousands of English, Welsh, Scottish and Irish settlers,
  soldiers and migrants from the 1600s to the 20th century."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** "British" is heard as "English only", leaving out the Welsh, Scottish and Irish.

### W-38 — Why Anglophones are called English Canadians
- **Fact:** That history is why English speakers are generally called English Canadians.
- **Where:** p. 20
- **Evidence:** "This helps explain why Anglophones (English speakers) are generally referred to as English
  Canadians."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** Taken to mean that English Canadians come from England.

## Section 6 — Diversity (p. 21)

### W-39 — Most Canadians were born here
- **Fact:** Most Canadians were born in Canada, and that has been true since the 1800s.
- **Where:** p. 21
- **Evidence:** "The majority of Canadians were born in this country and this has been true since the
  1800s."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** The phrase "land of immigrants" is read as meaning most Canadians were born elsewhere —
  which is the whole point of the sentence.

### W-40 — A land of immigrants
- **Fact:** Canada is often called a land of immigrants because over the past 200 years millions of
  newcomers have helped build and defend the country's way of life.
- **Where:** p. 21
- **Evidence:** "over the past 200 years, millions of newcomers have helped to build and defend our way of
  life."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** The phrase is thought to describe the last few decades only.

### W-41 — The largest ethnic groups
- **Fact:** The largest groups named are the English, French, Scottish, Irish, German, Italian, Chinese,
  Aboriginal, Ukrainian, Dutch, South Asian and Scandinavian.
- **Where:** p. 21
- **Evidence:** "The largest groups are the English, French, Scottish, Irish, German, Italian, Chinese,
  Aboriginal, Ukrainian, Dutch, South Asian and Scandinavian."
- **Stability:** **volatile** — a census ranking.
- **Misconception:** none sharp; the question is built as "which is not on the guide's list".

### W-42 — Where most immigrants have come from since the 1970s
- **Fact:** Since the 1970s, most immigrants have come from Asian countries.
- **Where:** p. 21
- **Evidence:** "Since the 1970s, most immigrants have come from Asian countries."
- **Stability:** **volatile** — a migration pattern that changes without notice.
- **Misconception:** Europe is assumed, from the earlier waves the guide describes.

### W-43 — Chinese languages at home
- **Fact:** Chinese languages are the second most-spoken at home after English in two of Canada's biggest
  cities: 13% in Vancouver and 7% in Toronto.
- **Where:** p. 21
- **Evidence:** "In Vancouver, 13% of the population speak Chinese languages at home; in Toronto, the number
  is 7%."
- **Stability:** **volatile** — census shares.
- **Misconception:** French is assumed to be second everywhere, including at home in Vancouver.

### W-44 — Religion
- **Fact:** The great majority of Canadians identify as Christians, and the largest single affiliation is
  Catholic, followed by various Protestant churches.
- **Where:** p. 21
- **Evidence:** "The largest religious affiliation is Catholic, followed by various Protestant churches."
- **Stability:** **volatile** — census shares.
- **Misconception:** Protestant is assumed to be the largest single affiliation.

### W-45 — Growing groups
- **Fact:** The numbers of Muslims, Jews, Hindus, Sikhs, members of other religions and people who state no
  religion are all growing.
- **Where:** p. 21
- **Evidence:** "as well as people who state “no religion” are also growing."
- **Stability:** **volatile** — a demographic trend.
- **Misconception:** "No religion" is left out of the list of things that are growing.

### W-46 — The state and faith communities
- **Fact:** In Canada the state has traditionally worked with faith communities to promote social welfare,
  harmony and mutual respect, to provide schools and health care, to resettle refugees, and to uphold
  religious freedom and freedom of conscience.
- **Where:** p. 21
- **Evidence:** "In Canada the state has traditionally partnered with faith communities to promote social
  welfare, harmony and mutual respect; to provide schools and health care; to resettle refugees; and to
  uphold religious freedom, religious expression and freedom of conscience."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** A strict separation is assumed, in which the state never works with religious
  organisations.

## Section 7 — Equality and a shared identity (p. 22)

### W-47 — Gay and lesbian Canadians
- **Fact:** Gay and lesbian Canadians have the full protection of the law and equal treatment under it,
  including access to civil marriage.
- **Where:** p. 22
- **Evidence:** "who enjoy the full protection of and equal treatment under the law, including access to
  civil marriage."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** Civil marriage is thought to be a provincial choice or a recent policy rather than
  equal treatment under the law.

### W-48 — A shared identity
- **Fact:** These diverse groups share a common Canadian identity and together make up today's multicultural
  society.
- **Where:** p. 22
- **Evidence:** "Together, these diverse groups, sharing a common Canadian identity, make up today's
  multicultural society."
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** Multiculturalism is read as many separate societies rather than one shared identity.

### W-49 — Black Loyalists
- **Fact:** In the 1780s, escaped slaves and free men and women of African origin fled to Canada from
  America, where slavery stayed legal until 1863.
- **Where:** p. 22, picture caption
- **Evidence:** "escaped slaves and freed men and women of African origin who in the 1780s fled to Canada
  from America, where slavery remained legal until 1863"
- **Stability:** stable
- **Misconception:** Assumed to belong to the Underground Railroad era of the mid-1800s only; the Loyalist
  migration is 80 years earlier.
