## Staleness

No `knownStaleness` entry in `content/sources/discover-canada.json` names this chapter, so `volatile` is my
judgement fact by fact. A dated historical event is stable — 1497 will not change with an election. Two
facts are marked volatile because they are present-tense statements about a standing institution rather
than about the past: H-69 (the RCMP as the national police force today) and H-93 (Canada as a leading
member of the Commonwealth today). Everything else in this chapter is stable.

**No monarch is named or gendered in any option or explanation.** This chapter names four historical
sovereigns. Three are needed only as scenery and are routed around: the 1670 Hudson's Bay Company charter is
answered by *the year and the company*, not by the king who granted it; the 1763 Royal Proclamation by the
document; the 1921 national colours by the fact. Only Francis I appears at all, in H-13, and he appears in a
prompt, never in an answer. This is not required by a gate for this chapter — the register's banned-term
lists attach to the governance chapters — it is the same discipline applied anyway, so that a future
register change cannot make a shipped answer wrong.

## Section 1 — Before and at contact (p. 23)

- **H-01** — Europeans called the peoples they met "Indians" because the first explorers believed they had
  reached the East Indies. *p23.* Evidence: "because the first explorers thought they had reached the East
  Indies." Stable. **Misconception:** the name is assumed to have been a self-description.
  *Terms note:* the guide's word appears only in the prompt, as the historical naming being explained.
- **H-02** — When Europeans explored Canada, the Huron-Wendat of the Great Lakes region, like the Iroquois,
  were farmers and hunters. *p23.* Evidence: "The Huron-Wendat of the Great Lakes region, like the Iroquois,
  were farmers and hunters." Stable. **Misconception:** every people is assumed to have been nomadic.
- **H-03** — The Cree and Dene of the Northwest were hunter-gatherers. *p23.* Evidence: "The Cree and Dene
  of the Northwest were hunter-gatherers." Stable. **Misconception:** confused with the farming peoples of
  the Great Lakes.
- **H-04** — The Sioux were nomadic and followed the bison herd. *p23.* Evidence: "The Sioux were nomadic,
  following the bison (buffalo) herd." Stable. **Misconception:** the bison is associated with the Cree or
  the Dene instead.
- **H-05** — West Coast peoples preserved fish by drying and smoking it. *p23.* Evidence: "West Coast
  natives preserved fish by drying and smoking." Stable. **Misconception:** salting or freezing is guessed.
- **H-06** — The Inuit lived off Arctic wildlife. *p23.* Evidence: "The Inuit lived off Arctic wildlife."
  Stable. Used only as an option in H-04's set, never as a past-tense assertion of its own (§9.3).
- **H-07 — FLAGGED, NOT AUTHORED.** *p23,* the sentence beginning "Warfare was common among Aboriginal
  groups". A question here would put a broad characterisation of peoples in the correct-answer slot. There
  is no faithful version that is not that, and softening it is the other thing `OQ-REVIEW-9` forbids. Said
  plainly and skipped.
- **H-08** — Large numbers of Aboriginal people died of European diseases they had no immunity to. *p23.*
  Evidence: "Large numbers of Aboriginals died of European diseases to which they lacked immunity." Stable.
  **Misconception:** the deaths are attributed entirely to warfare.
- **H-09** — In the first 200 years of coexistence, Aboriginal peoples and Europeans built strong economic,
  religious and military bonds, which laid the foundations of Canada. *p23.* Evidence: "Aboriginals and
  Europeans formed strong economic, religious and military bonds in the first 200 years of coexistence which
  laid the foundations of Canada." Stable. **Misconception:** the whole contact period is imagined as
  uninterrupted conflict.
- **H-10** — John Cabot, an Italian who had moved to England, was the first to map Canada's Atlantic shore,
  landing on Newfoundland or Cape Breton Island in 1497. English settlement did not begin until 1610. *p23,
  picture caption.* Evidence: "setting foot on Newfoundland or Cape Breton Island in 1497 and claiming the
  New Founde Land for England. English settlement did not begin until 1610". Stable. **Misconception:**
  Cabot is assumed to be Spanish or to have settled the island at once.
